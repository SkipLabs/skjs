Remaining limits

  • Reactions: then, catch and finally keep N-bounded contracts: a reaction's payload must be stable.
  • Thenable adoption is refused on principle: await of a PromiseLike, and async return of a fresh object, would call a then the type does not describe.
  • Bounds naming another binder, such as T extends U, get no stable-bound shortcut: Awaited<T> stays apart from T.
  • Higher-order uses need generic function types, which are outside the fragment.
  • Further rules would cover: values a Promise executor resolves, a second reaction on an already-settled promise, thenables that only resolve to themselves, and promises that can never fulfill.
  • Listed gaps: explicit catch<string>, source Promise copies.
